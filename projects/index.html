Building a Connected Trail Network

Not just a bike park. NAMBA is building an interconnected trail network across the Niseko region. From our flagship Twin Peaks to new trails at Grand Hirafu and Hanazono, each project is part of a single connected map.

The Niseko Trail Masterplan

The reason Niseko's trail network works is coordination. Rather than each resort or landowner building trails independently, NAMBA develops a regional masterplan that ensures every project contributes to a single, connected network.
Without coordination, the result would be duplicated difficulty levels, disconnected networks, and gaps in the mix that riders need. The masterplan prevents this.

  • Connectivity

    Every park and trail area connected, enabling riders to move between areas in a single session.

  • Diversity

    The right mix of trail difficulty across the region, from green through double-black, so every rider finds what they need.

  • Distance targets

    Planned growth toward a total trail distance that puts Niseko alongside the major MTB destinations.

  • Unified experience

    Consistent signage, wayfinding, and experience design across every park.

  • Efficient use of terrain

    The right trail types in the right terrain, avoiding duplication across parks.

Masterplan overview: Map showing the planned trail network across the Niseko region

This masterplan is what makes NAMBA essential to resort, landowner, and government partners. NAMBA doesn't just build trails. We design the network that makes Niseko's mountains work for summer.

Alpine Trails & the Future

Beyond the current network, NAMBA is exploring alpine trails that would connect Niseko's peaks and open terrain currently off-limits to mountain bikers. Combined with the Mt Yotei loop and continued expansion at the resort parks, the long-term picture is a trail system on par with the best.
