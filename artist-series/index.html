He channels the primal energy of line and form, using shapes as building blocks to create allegorical worlds.

His works weave together striking geometric patterns, natural forms, and contrasting motifs, unfolding like stories. Through timeless imagery that connects past and future, he explores the profound relationship between humanity and nature.

The harmony he achieves—between strong, structured backdrops and soft, flowing motifs—creates a captivating balance. His distinctive depictions of forests, expressed through layered compositions, intricate detail, and nuanced color, invite viewers into a world of depth and resonance.

Born in 1976 and based in Sapporo, he graduated from the Hokkaido College of Art and Design and began his career in 2008. He has served as the visual director for the Tobiu Art Festival in Shiraoi Town, and his practice spans solo exhibitions, collaborative projects such as the Silkio Project, Geso Portraito, Stopero Pinks, and the spring group show Haru Urara. Beyond the gallery, his work includes live painting at outdoor music festivals, murals for shops and public spaces, commissioned pieces, and book cover illustrations.

I am an artist, mountaineer and snowboarder travelling across the world to find inspiration for my paintings. I want to bring the feeling of being in the mountains onto canvas.

Seeing the potential for serious alpine style bike parks in Japan is really exciting and the work NAMBA and other Japanese mountain bikers are doing to make this possible and build an international mountain biking community makes me optimistic about the future if mountain biking in Japan. Born and raised in Birmingham, England, but made in Sapporo.

Despite being an avid sketcher as a kid, I left art behind as a teenager with school and University leading me elsewhere. In my early 20s, I discovered vector art and suddenly my own limitations with a pencil were quickly forgotten and I’d entered a new world that I’d never leave. Influenced by travel posters and national parks illustrations, I became enamoured with the idea of using art and design as a means of exploration, creating countless badges and posters for worlds that don’t exist, or ones that were just of reach. But living in Hokkaido, the world I was most interested in was just outside my window.

Moving to Sapporo fresh out of University was bound to be a life-altering experience but I could never have anticipated the extent to which it’s affected me. Now my permanent home, Hokkaido serves as endless inspiration for my artwork and designs while working with local businesses on their branding and identity, or creating a set of National Parks style designs for Hokkaido’s Mountains and other notable landmarks.

I'm an Aussie designer and artist living in Niseko area since 2013. These days, I'm living on the Iwanai mountainside looking out to the ocean with my wife Tomomi who is the founder/chef at Layer Niseko in Kutchan. I currently share my personal art under ‘Mtnsea’, on instagram but it started as an idea for a surf/snow artists collective, was then an online t-shirt and prints shop but is nowadays more of a lowkey visual diary— separate from my commercial work at Sandls Design (where I am proud to have created the Twin Peaks logo). Been drawing since I was a kid back in Brisbane, always lost in books, the bush and exploring means of understanding and escapism. During COVID, I started screenprinting at home—still a total beginner, but enjoying the process.

With art and life in general, I find peace by trying to surrender my fears and expectations, though the ego picks them right back up again, so I use painting as a meditative practice. I explore notions of identity and spirit, following hunches about hidden connections, our true nature and meaning. The past five years of sobriety has allowed me to transform my life, working through trauma and addiction, I've rebuilt relationships with family back home, and I’m starting to explore who I am or what life can be through a totally different lense. I'm passionate about supporting others who are struggling, looking to help share what was given to me by mental health services, online communities and friends and family both here and abroad. So if you're struggling, please reach out.

For all its natural beauty and mix of people and activities, Hokkaido can be isolating, it's part of its allure. But I've learned there's a world of difference between feeling lonely and living in peaceful solitude. These days, I'm grateful for my quiet creative life and keen to give back to this place that's become home. I also want to thank NAMBA for all their work on and off the mountain, building community and making trails.

- name: Peter Martin # The Rainbow Captain title: Making Art & Stuff in Hokkaido img: /assets/images/artists/peter-martin/avatar.png header: /assets/images/artists/peter-martin/header.png instagram: therainbowcaptain facebook: null # links: https://59niseko.com work: - /assets/images/artists/peter-martin/artwork-01.png - /assets/images/artists/peter-martin/artwork-02.jpg - /assets/images/artists/peter-martin/artwork-03.jpg - /assets/images/artists/peter-martin/artwork-04.jpg - /assets/images/artists/peter-martin/artwork-05.jpg - /assets/images/artists/peter-martin/artwork-06.jpg - /assets/images/artists/peter-martin/artwork-07.jpg bio: |

The Rainbow Captain is a Hokkaido-based artist, originally from New York. After relocating to Japan in the mid-1990s, he spent over a decade immersed in Tokyo’s fashion industry and also worked extensively as an art director and production designer, creating sets, props, and visual environments for international commercials and short films. In recent years, he left the intensity of urban life behind and settled in rural Hokkaido, where he now devotes himself to a more personal and exploratory creative practice. His work reflects a unique sensibility shaped by decades of living as a Westerner in Japan—not merely as an outsider looking in, but through sustained cultural immersion. This perspective informs a body of work that offers a distinct interpretation alongside both local understandings and international perspectives of Japanese visual culture.

Drawing inspiration from vintage Japanese illustration, mid-century design, and the everyday aesthetics of the Showa era, The Rainbow Captain explores pattern, repetition, and cultural iconography through a distinctly personal lens.

He also produces a line of handmade omiyage—wearable objects and collectible trinkets created in limited runs using analog methods, blending humor, craftsmanship, and nostalgia. These small-batch creations are released periodically, each one carrying its own sense of timing, intention, and play.

Keep an eye out—new projects, collaborations, and one-of-a-kind creations are always on the horizon.

Scott is a multidisciplinary artist, who has spent time working as a glass blower, painter, illustrator, digital designer and tattooer.

Scott was drawn to the American traditional tattooing genre when in 2007 and 2008 he went through a traditional tattoo apprenticeship in Jacksonville, Florida.

Since 2008 his primary focus has been on American traditional tattooing.

Originally from Colorado, he moved to Niseko in 2019 to start a family with his wife, who is from Kutchan. This is why he chose to build his tattoo studio, 59 Niseko, in the heart of downtown Kutchan.
