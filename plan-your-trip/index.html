Your guide to riding Niseko

Trails, onsen, and the volcanic landscape of western Hokkaido. Here's what you need to plan a mountain biking trip to Niseko.

Rider on trail with Mt Yotei in background, summer landscape

When to visit

The riding season runs from late May to late October, with conditions varying by month.
  • June: Trails opening, fresh green. Some higher trails may still have snow patches early in the month.
  • July & August: Peak summer. Warm, lush, occasional rain. All trails open. Hanazono opens summer 2026.
  • September: The sweet spot. Cooler temperatures, autumn colours starting, trails in prime condition. Biggest events month.
  • October: Autumn colours, quieter trails, crisp air. Some trails close late in the month as snow approaches.

Getting here

Niseko is in western Hokkaido, Japan, approximately 2 to 2.5 hours from Sapporo.
  • By air: Fly into New Chitose Airport (CTS). Direct flights from Tokyo (1h45), Osaka, and seasonal international services from Asia-Pacific cities.
  • From the airport: Rental car is the most convenient option (~2 hours to Niseko). Bus services also run to Niseko (Whiteliner, Chuo Bus ~3 hours). There is no direct train; the JR line goes via Otaru and Kutchan.
  • Getting around Niseko: A car is strongly recommended. Twin Peaks, Grand Hirafu, and Hanazono are spread across the area. Public transport is limited in summer.

Bike rentals

Several shops in the Niseko area offer mountain bike rentals, from hardtails to full-suspension enduro rigs. Local bike shops such as EZObase can help you find the right bike for Niseko's terrain.
If you're bringing your own bike, bike bags are accepted on domestic flights and most international carriers. Check your airline's sports equipment policy.

Where to stay

Niseko has a range of accommodation, from resort hotels to small pensions and self-catered apartments. Most sits around Hirafu village, within easy driving distance of every riding area.
Many of our community partners offer accommodation, and supporting them supports the trails you ride.
Stay somewhere with an onsen (hot spring bath) if you can. A post-ride soak in mineral spring water is part of the local rhythm.

What to pack

  • Essential: Helmet (mandatory at Twin Peaks), gloves, appropriate footwear, water bottle.
  • Recommended: Full-face helmet for advanced trails, knee pads, eye protection, rain jacket (weather changes fast).
  • Nice to have: GPS device or phone mount (trails are well-marked but useful for gravel routes), hydration pack, insect repellent (summer in Hokkaido!).
  • If riding Twin Peaks: All you need is a bike and a helmet. Trails are free, no registration required.

Riding for every level

  • Beginners: Skills parks at the bike park bases, balance-bike loops for kids, and the Forest Loop at Hanazono (opening summer 2026).
  • Intermediate: The majority of Twin Peaks trails, plus the blue downhill course opening at Hanazono. Flow, singletrack, and a growing network across the region.
  • Advanced / Expert: Technical singletrack and natural-terrain lines at Twin Peaks. The kind of riding that brought Loic Bruni here in 2025.

Beyond the bike

Niseko isn't just trails. It's a destination for food and wellness in its own right.
  • Onsen: Natural hot springs surround Niseko, fed by the volcanic landscape. Several are outdoor baths with mountain views. The local way to end a ride.

Outdoor onsen with mountain views: where a Niseko ride ends

  • Food: Hokkaido is Japan's farm belt. Farm-to-table restaurants, fresh seafood, craft beer (including the Yotei Brewing x Twin Peaks collaboration), and Hokkaido's renowned dairy.
  • Activities: When you're not riding: hiking, rafting, kayaking, fishing, or exploring the volcanic landscape around Mt Yotei.
