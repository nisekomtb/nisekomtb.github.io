## Launch Control, described

Tom's own account, and the most useful thing in the second recording.

**Where it sits.** Launch Control starts from the same hub that gives you the middle section of
Cruise Control, Taki Trail, or the continuation of the middle section of Shogun Syndicate. You
can come down Cruise Control and detour off to it. Launch Control rejoins the end of the middle
section of Cruise Control. A dedicated climb trail, **Repeat Offender**, returns you to that
hub, so you can lap Launch Control or middle Cruise Control indefinitely. That is what people
actually do: get to that hub and lap the jump line. They exit either via the end of Cruise
Control or by riding over to the lower section of Shogun Syndicate.

**The run itself, in order.** A short climb first to gain elevation. At the top of the climb
there is a genuinely good view of Mount Yotei. A small stop zone. Then a small jump, then three
medium jumps, then some berms and doubles, then one big switchback berm that lets you carry
full speed into a higher medium jump, then a right-hander and a left-hander, then one long
line of several jumps and doubles running at the big jump at the end. After the big jump, a
couple more corners, a double, and you are back at the hub.

**The design idea, which is the line worth using:** if you can clear every jump in that final
line, you know you have the speed for the big one at the end. The jump line calibrates you as
you ride it.

## The women's category call

The whip off ran on the big jump at the bottom for the men's categories. The women's ran on the
**third and largest of the three medium jumps** instead. Reason: some women wanted to enter but
were wary of the last big jump, so moving the category to the medium jumps let more people
enter. Tom's framing throughout is that getting people to enter mattered more than policing
what counted as a whip.

Note this refines the first interview, where he described it as "the bigger of the small jumps".
It is the biggest of the *medium* jumps.

## The party train

It did happen. During the competition riders went one at a time with about a minute between
them, once the jump was confirmed clear. The party train was everyone going at once, so **any
frame showing more than one rider in the air at the same time is the party train.**

## The crowd

About 50, and Tom's estimate. Locals, parents, the kids who entered. The surprise was that
people came purely to watch: they had expected volunteers plus a couple of NAMBA people.

## The end of the day

Frame 30 is the winners on top of the big jump. Mood good, tunes still playing. Some stayed to
help pack up, then everyone rode down to the bottom together.

## The closing argument, for the CTA

Tom's answer to "why come next August", nearly verbatim: it is a chance to see people hitting
these jumps properly, and a chance to look around Twin Peaks **without committing to riding a
bike**. You can walk up to the event and walk back down again and see what the place is like.
That part of the hill has great views, and you can see Yotei.

That last point is the strongest thing in the answer and does not appear anywhere on the site:
the event is a way to visit Twin Peaks without being a mountain biker.

photographer shot in bursts, so several frames are the same instant one or two seconds
apart. Four of the eight candidates picked off contact sheets turned out to duplicate a
frame already published, and were only caught by rendering them in the gallery and
looking:

| 55 | 31 | 31. Rider higher and closer, and the foreground rider is grinning at the camera |
| 58 | 30 | 30. Same group photo, tighter, faces larger, flags framing it |

**A 440px contact-sheet cell cannot separate a burst duplicate from the frame already in
the story.** Both cells read as "three women with bikes" or "group behind the banner".
The check that works is to place the candidate in its gallery, render it, and look at it
beside the published frame. Do that before writing the alt text, not after.

Second rule from the same pass: **compare a candidate against what is ALREADY PUBLISHED,
not just against the other candidates.** Every one of the five above passed as a good
photograph on its own. They failed on redundancy, which is invisible when the take is all
you are looking at.

Frames 53, 55, 56 and 58 are ingested with alt text and left in place, alongside 9, 13,
16, 40 and 41 which were already unused. They ship in the Pages artifact without being
referenced by any page. Worth a clear-out once the story is final, along with the triage
