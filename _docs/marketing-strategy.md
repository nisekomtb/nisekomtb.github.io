# NAMBA Marketing Strategy 2026–2027
**Niseko Area Mountain Bike Association**
*Prepared: March 2026 — v3*

## Executive Summary

NAMBA is not just a bike park. It is becoming the de facto mountain bike development authority for the entire Niseko region — building and managing Twin Peaks, consulting on Grand Hirafu's bike park expansion, opening Hanazono's first trails this summer, designing unified signage systems across parks, and planning the ambitious Mt Yotei loop trail. No other organization in Japan is doing this at this scale, and no other nonprofit in the country builds free-to-ride trails.

This positioning — **"Building trails and community"** — is NAMBA's public identity and the anchor for all marketing. The strategy below is designed to communicate this broader story to two critical audiences: international riders who will visit and become ambassadors, and corporate sponsors whose investment makes every trail possible.

**Strategic priorities (ranked):**
1. Attract corporate sponsors and partners (trail sponsorship is the funding model — trails only get built if sponsored)
2. Build brand awareness internationally (Asia-Pacific + Global/Western MTB markets)
3. Increase rider visits across the Niseko trail network
4. Grow individual memberships and merchandise revenue

**Key context shaping this plan:**
- NAMBA manages multiple projects: Twin Peaks (20 trails, 21km+), Grand Hirafu bike park consulting, Hanazono trails (construction started 2025, opening July 2026 — 4,500m lift-access flow trail: Niseko's first lift-accessed top-to-bottom blue trail + skills park + link trail), Mt Yotei loop (planning), future alpine trail concepts
- 2024 data: 7,500+ visitors to Twin Peaks, 230 peak daily visits, 1,200+ event attendees, 100% trail funding success rate, Dirty Dames women's program with 200+ participants
- 2026 trail builds pending sponsorship: Green Flow Trail (¥8.45M) and Lower Green Climb (¥13M)
- Dual sponsorship structure: Community Partners (local businesses, ¥50K–500K+) and MTB Brand Partners (industry brands, ¥100K–1M+, cash+product with co-branded event rights). Website currently only shows Community Partner tiers.
- Pinkbike Trail of the Month article confirmed with strong imagery
- Tanuki Productions handles photography and manages @nisekomtb on Instagram/Facebook
- Artist Series merch with global dropshipping — launched late 2025, needs traffic growth. New summer designs coming, plus a Yotei Brewing x Twin Peaks custom beer can collaboration (Artist Series artist Joe Scott designing the label)
- Influencer hosting program planned for Summer 2027 (potential grant funding)
- All content to be bilingual (EN/JP) by default, with potential for additional Asian languages
- NAMBA has an existing LinkedIn page (linkedin.com/company/nisekomtb)

## 1. Positioning & Messaging

### The Positioning Shift

The current website presents NAMBA primarily as the operator of Twin Peaks Bike Park. The reality is much bigger: NAMBA is the driving force behind mountain biking across the entire Niseko area, working with resorts, landowners, and government to build an interconnected trail network. The website and all communications should reflect this broader role.

**Current perception:** "NAMBA runs a free bike park in Niseko"
**Target perception:** "NAMBA is building Asia's premier mountain bike destination — an interconnected network of world-class trails across the Niseko region"

### Unique Value Proposition

> **"Building trails and community — transforming Niseko into Asia's premier mountain bike destination through free, world-class trails designed by Swiss craftsmen, an interconnected network spanning multiple resorts, and a thriving riding community."**

What makes NAMBA's story unique (and unsurpassed in Asia):

- **Japan's largest free-access, nonprofit-built trail network** — While other NPOs and clubs in Japan build or maintain trails (Minami Alps MTB Club, Minoh MTB Friends, KAMBA Kobe, Trail Cutter), none offer what NAMBA does: 21km+ of professionally designed trails, open to anyone, any day during the season, with no membership, registration, briefing, guided tour, or donation required. Minami Alps requires a ¥12,000 annual membership and orientation to ride their main trails; Minoh opens only on scheduled days with registration and member supervision; KAMBA Kobe (opened May 2025) currently offers a single 425m loop; Trail Cutter operates as a paid guided-tour service on private land. NAMBA's Twin Peaks is free in the most complete sense — show up, ride, no strings attached — and at a scale none of these others approach.
- **Multi-park, interconnected network vision** — not a single bike park, but an ecosystem: Twin Peaks + Grand Hirafu + Hanazono + Mt Yotei loop + future alpine trails, all with unified signage and experience design
- **Swiss-designed trails** — the Allegra partnership provides world-class credibility
- **Niseko's global brand** — existing name recognition, luxury infrastructure, international flights. NAMBA extends the brand from winter to year-round
- **Community-first model** — Dirty Dames, Soil Searching volunteer days, events, artist collaborations. This isn't a commercial operation; it's a movement
- **100% trail funding success rate** — every trail has been community and industry-funded. This track record is a powerful trust signal for new sponsors
- **Unified signage and experience design across parks.** NAMBA designs the trail signage for Twin Peaks, Grand Hirafu, and Hanazono, creating a cohesive user experience across the entire region. The Hanazono signage (visible in the pitch deck with the Hanazono brand integrated into NAMBA's signage system) demonstrates that NAMBA is the regional standard-setter for trail infrastructure. This is also the physical foundation for Bike Niseko's future identity — riders experience one connected network, not isolated parks
- **Consulting and regulatory expertise** — NAMBA has demonstrated the ability to deliver projects within complex, multi-stakeholder environments with strict regulations, including building within Hoanrin-class preservation forest. This is a capability that positions NAMBA as the essential partner for any resort or landowner in the region wanting to develop trails
- **Multi-use trail vision** — NAMBA's scope extends beyond MTB to include hiking, trail running, and multi-use trail integration. The Hanazono project includes plans to activate hiking trail networks via lift access and connect to the Niseko Trail system, broadening the appeal and stakeholder base beyond just mountain bikers

### Core Narrative

*Niseko's legendary powder made it a world-class winter destination. Now, NAMBA — the organization behind Japan's largest free-access, nonprofit-built trail network — is writing the summer chapter. From the Swiss-designed Twin Peaks Bike Park to new trails at Grand Hirafu and Hanazono, NAMBA is building an interconnected network that will make Niseko Asia's premier mountain bike destination. What snowsports did for Niseko's winters, mountain biking will do for its summers.*

The website is NAMBA's single most important marketing asset, and it currently underrepresents both the scale of what NAMBA is doing and the data that makes the sponsor case compelling.

The pitch deck PDF contains powerful data (7,500+ visitors, 230 peak daily, 100% funding record, year-by-year trail growth maps) that is invisible to anyone who doesn't receive a direct presentation. This information needs to live on the website.

**Current site architecture analysis:**

The sitemap reveals 43 URLs. The structure is solid for Twin Peaks operations but has three major gaps:

1. **No "Niseko MTB ecosystem" story** — The site presents Twin Peaks in isolation. There's no mention of Grand Hirafu consulting, Hanazono trail opening, Mt Yotei loop, or the broader vision. A visitor has no idea NAMBA is building a region-wide network.

3. **Impact data is buried in PDFs** — The pitch deck contains the most compelling arguments for sponsorship (visitor numbers, growth trajectory, year-by-year trail maps), but none of this is on the website.

### Channel 2: Instagram & YouTube (Visual Storytelling)
**Priority: HIGH — Primary awareness driver**

**Why this channel:** Mountain biking is one of the most visual sports on earth. Instagram is where the international MTB community lives, YouTube is where trip-planning research happens, and you already have Tanuki Productions creating strong content.

**Instagram strategy — @nisekomtb (primary account):**

Tanuki Productions manages this account, which is good — professional consistency matters. The strategic shift should be:

- **Broaden beyond Twin Peaks** — Feature content from Hirafu, Hanazono (when it opens), and gravel events. This reinforces NAMBA's regional role, not just single-park identity.
- **Shift mix toward trail POV and lifestyle content** — Event announcements are important but shouldn't dominate. The feed should make someone in Sydney or Singapore think "I need to ride there."
- **Post 3–4x/week during season** (June–October), 1–2x/week off-season

- **English-first captions with Japanese** — given international audience priority
- **Strategic hashtags:** #mtbjapan #niseko #japanmtb #trailbuilding #mountainbikejapan #hokkaido #bikepark #japantravel

**@twinpeaksbikepark: recommendation.** Don't invest in growing this as a separate managed account right now. Instead, Tanuki should be responsible for collaborating relevant Twin Peaks content from @nisekomtb to this account, keeping it active with trail status updates, openings, and key trail content. This keeps the account alive without splitting creative effort. Focus budget on making @nisekomtb the definitive MTB account for Japan.

**YouTube strategy:**

YouTube is where you build the long-form assets that compound over time. Priority content:

1. **Hero brand video (3 to 5 min)** — Your instinct is right: evolve beyond just a riding edit. 3. **"Niseko MTB Trip Planner"** — The definitive 5–8 minute guide for someone planning a visit. Embed on the Plan Your Trip page.

4. **Trail building documentary** — This is a genuinely compelling idea. NAMBA's story — behind Japan's largest free-access trail network, the Allegra partnership, the vision of transforming Niseko — is inherently cinematic. This could be a 15–20 minute piece that serves as your ultimate brand asset: homepage feature, sponsor meetings, media pitches, festival submissions. ### Channel 4: Google Ad Grants (Free Search Ads for Nonprofits)
**Priority: HIGH — Up to $10,000/month in free Google Ads**

**Why this channel:** As a registered NPO, NAMBA qualifies for the Google Ad Grants program, which provides up to $10,000 USD (~¥1.5M) per month in free Google Search advertising. This is essentially a free, always-on SEO accelerator — while your organic content builds over time, Google Ads can put NAMBA at the top of search results immediately for your most important keywords.

This is an enormous advantage that most organizations pay heavily for. Combined with the blog and Plan Your Trip page, Ad Grants can drive significant international traffic from day one of the season.

**How to maximize it:**

- **Ensure your account is active and compliant** — Google Ad Grants has specific rules: max $2 bid per keyword (unless using Smart Bidding), must maintain 5% click-through rate, must have valid conversion tracking. If the account exists but hasn't been actively managed, audit it now.
- **Priority campaign: "Visit Niseko MTB"** — Target rider-intent keywords: "mountain biking Japan," "Niseko summer activities," "bike park Japan," "MTB Hokkaido," "Japan cycling holiday." Send traffic to /plan-your-trip/ page.
- **Priority campaign: "Sponsor NAMBA"** — Target sponsor-intent keywords: "sponsor mountain biking Japan," "Niseko sponsorship," "outdoor brand sponsorship Japan." Send traffic to /get-involved/partner/ page. Lower volume but extremely high value per click.
- **Priority campaign: "Twin Peaks Bike Park"** — Brand and destination keywords: "Twin Peaks bike park," "Niseko bike park," "free bike park Japan." Send to /twin-peaks/ page.
- **Seasonal campaign: Events** — Promote specific events during registration periods.
- **Landing pages matter** — Ad Grants performance depends heavily on landing page quality. This is another reason the /plan-your-trip/ and enhanced /partner/ pages are Tier 1 priorities: they're not just for organic visitors, they're the conversion destinations for your free ad spend.
- **Language targeting** — Run separate campaigns in English (targeting international MTB markets) and Japanese (targeting domestic riders). Consider testing campaigns in Traditional Chinese for Hong Kong/Taiwan market.

**Important:** The $10,000/month is a cap, not a guarantee — actual spend depends on keyword competition and ad quality. But even at 30–50% utilization, this could deliver thousands of qualified visitors per month for free.

## 11. Brand Architecture: NAMBA + Bike Niseko (Future)

### The Strategic Case

NAMBA's website is currently asked to do two fundamentally different jobs: (1) represent the nonprofit association — trail building, community, sponsorship, advocacy — and (2) serve as the visitor-facing tourism portal for mountain biking in Niseko. These are different audiences, different tones, and different conversion goals. As the Niseko MTB ecosystem grows (Twin Peaks + Hirafu + Hanazono + Yotei loop + future trails), this tension will only increase.

**Bike Niseko** resolves this by creating a clean brand split:

### Brand Architecture

**NAMBA (namba.ngo)** — *"Building trails and community"*
The nonprofit mountain bike association. The organization that makes everything happen.

| Attribute | NAMBA |
|-----------|-------|
| **Core identity** | Nonprofit trail builder, community organizer, MTB development authority |
| **Primary audiences** | Corporate sponsors, resort/government partners, local riding community, media |
| **Key content** | About/mission/story, sponsorship & partnership, impact data, team, projects overview, community events (dig days, Dirty Dames, group rides), jobs, membership, Artist Series shop, documentary, blog (organizational stories, trail building updates, community news) |
| **Tone** | Grassroots, purpose-driven, community, authentic. "We build trails and bring people together." |
| **Conversion goals** | Sponsor inquiries, membership signups, merch sales, volunteer signups, donations |
| **Social** | @nisekomtb (Instagram/Facebook), LinkedIn, YouTube (organizational/documentary content) |

**Bike Niseko (bikeniseko.com or similar)** — *"Ride Niseko"* (working concept)
The consumer-facing destination brand for all cycling in Niseko.

| Attribute | Bike Niseko |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Core identity** | The definitive guide to cycling in the Niseko region — MTB, gravel, road, e-bike |
| **Primary audiences** | International MTB tourists (Asia-Pacific + Western), domestic Japanese riders, cycling tourists, families |
| **Key content** | Interactive trail & route maps, trail guides (all parks), "Plan Your Trip" (accommodation, rentals, transport, when to visit), events calendar (races, competitions, festivals), guide/tour operator directory, conditions & trail status, area guide (food, onsen, activities), photo/video gallery |
| **Tone** | Polished destination marketing, aspirational, practical. "Everything you need to ride Niseko." |
| **Conversion goals** | Trip planning engagement, event registrations, guide/operator bookings, accommodation referrals, newsletter signups |
| **Social** | Potentially @bikeniseko (Instagram), YouTube (trail guides, trip planning, riding edits) |

### How the Brands Work Together

The relationship is symbiotic, not competitive:

- **NAMBA builds it, Bike Niseko promotes it.** NAMBA creates the trails, the network, the infrastructure, the community. Bike Niseko packages that for the consumer as a destination experience.
- **Bike Niseko drives visitors, NAMBA monetizes them for trail building.** More visitors = stronger impact data = more compelling sponsor pitches = more trails get funded.
- **Cross-linking is natural.** Bike Niseko's trail pages link to NAMBA for "support the trails that make this possible." NAMBA's site links to Bike Niseko for "plan your visit."
- **Sponsors appear in both contexts.** A trail sponsor's logo appears on NAMBA's partner page (organizational context) AND on Bike Niseko's trail guide for that trail (consumer context). Double exposure, same investment.
- **Bike Niseko becomes a community unifier.** Resorts, accommodation providers, bike shops, restaurants, tour operators — businesses that might not see themselves as "mountain bike sponsors" can easily become "Bike Niseko destination partners." This potentially opens an entirely new tier of commercial partnerships beyond NAMBA's current trail sponsorship model.

## 14. Membership Strategy & Nintei NPO Path

### Current State

Memberships are required by NAMBA's articles of association. Current tiers: Adult ¥5,000, Youth ¥2,500, Child free, Family ¥9,000. The product is acknowledged as needing improvement, but the pricing structure can be changed without amending articles.

### Strategic Goal: Nintei NPO (認定NPO法人) Status

Growing membership numbers is important beyond revenue — it's a pathway to nintei NPO status, which would allow donors to receive tax deductions on contributions. This significantly increases the attractiveness of both donations and potentially larger sponsor contributions.

**What this means for marketing:** Membership growth should be framed not just as "support us" but as "join us" — building the membership base that demonstrates community value and unlocks tax-deductible status for everyone's benefit.

### Membership Product — Current Reality

The membership product currently offers one core benefit: **you directly support the trails you ride.** Additional tangible perks are not feasible right now, and that's okay. For the right audience — riders who love what NAMBA is building and want to see it continue — that's enough. The key is framing it well.

**Current framing (on the website):** The membership page is already warm and fun ("GET THAT WARM FUZZY FEELING!"). Keep this tone. But sharpen the impact message: make it concrete what a membership funds.

**Recommended messaging:**
- "Your ¥5,000 membership funds [X] meters of trail maintenance" (or equivalent tangible cost)
- "Join [X] members backing Japan's largest free-access trail network"
- "Every member brings us closer to nintei NPO status — unlocking tax-deductible donations for everyone"

**What to show on the membership page:**
- Total member count (displayed prominently — social proof)
- Impact framing: what memberships collectively fund
- Community photos: people riding, digging, socializing
- The existing fun tone — keep it

**Future benefits (bank these ideas for when capacity allows):**
When NAMBA is ready to add tangible member benefits, consider: member discount coordination with sponsor businesses, priority event registration, exclusive group rides or events, digital member badge, "member since [year]" recognition, and a name on a supporters section of the website. These can be introduced incrementally as the membership base and sponsor relationships grow — no need to launch everything at once.

**Twin Peaks — Full depth at /twin-peaks/ (NAMBA-managed, top-level)**

## 17. Homepage Strategy

### Who Lands on the Homepage?

The homepage serves four distinct visitors, each with different intent:

1. **Riders discovering NAMBA** — "What is this place? Can I ride here?" → Need to quickly understand what NAMBA is and get to /where-to-ride/ or /twin-peaks/
2. **Potential sponsors/partners** — "Is this worth investing in?" → Need to see scale, credibility, and impact, then get to /partner/
3. **Community members** — "What's happening? How do I get involved?" → Need events, membership, volunteer opportunities
4. **Media/researchers** — "What's the story here?" → Need the narrative, data, and press resources

The homepage must serve all four in under 10 seconds of scrolling. It's a routing page, not an information dump.

### Homepage Structure (Top to Bottom)

**1. Hero section — the emotional hook**
- Hero video (the new Tanuki Productions piece: riding + trail building + community + landscape)
- Overlay: NAMBA logo + tagline "Building trails and community" + one-line UVP
- Single primary CTA: "Discover the trails" → /where-to-ride/
- Secondary CTA: "Support the mission" → /partner/

**2. "What is NAMBA?" — the 10-second story**
- 2–3 sentences max: who you are, what you do, why it matters
- Something like: "NAMBA is a volunteer-led nonprofit building Japan's largest free-access mountain bike trail network in Niseko, Hokkaido. From a single trail in 2022 to 21km+ across three resorts — we're transforming Niseko into Asia's premier mountain bike destination."
- Link: "Our story" → /about/

**3. Impact stats bar — the proof**
- 4–5 headline numbers displayed visually (icons or bold typography):
  - "21km+ of trail" / "11,500+ riders in 2025" / "50+ partners" / "20,000+ volunteer hours" / "100% trail funding success"
- These numbers do the heavy lifting for every audience: riders see scale, sponsors see traction, media see a story
- Link: "See our impact" → /impact/

**4. Where to ride — the product**
- Visual cards for Twin Peaks, Grand Hirafu, Hanazono (with "opening July 2026" badge), and the Yotei loop teaser
- Each card: striking photo, name, key stat (trail count or "lift access"), and link
- Section heading: "Where to ride" or "Ride Niseko"

**5. Current news / what's happening — the pulse**
- 2–3 items maximum, auto-updating or manually curated:
  - Latest blog post or announcement (e.g., "Pinkbike Trail of the Month feature")
  - Next upcoming event (with date and link to /events/)
  - Trail status summary (e.g., "Twin Peaks: Open — 18 of 20 trails clear")

- This drives traffic to the shop without making the homepage feel commercial

## 19. Partnership Framing & URL Migration

### The Reframe: Sponsorship → Partnership

The language shift from "sponsor" to "partner" is strategically important:

- **"Sponsor"** implies: you give money, you get a logo. Transactional. One-directional. Charity.
- **"Partner"** implies: shared goals, mutual benefit, collaboration. Strategic. Two-directional. Investment.

NAMBA's model is genuinely a partnership — sponsors get permanent brand presence on physical infrastructure, media exposure, co-branded event rights, commercial operator passes, and association with a growing international destination. Framing this as partnership elevates the conversation and justifies higher price points, especially for the MTB Brand Partner tier.

### URL Migration

**Current:** /get-involved/partner/
**Recommended:** /partner/

Rationale: Shorter, cleaner, more direct. It signals that partnership is a first-class section of the site, not a sub-item under "Get Involved." The /get-involved/ path can remain as the hub for membership, donations, and volunteering — but partnership is significant enough (and the #1 priority) to have its own top-level URL.

**Navigation update:**
- Current nav: Get Involved → Become a Partner
- New nav: "Partner with us" as a top-level nav item (or "Partnership"), sitting alongside Twin Peaks, Where to Ride, About, Events, Shop
- This gives partnership equal weight in the navigation to riding — which reflects its strategic importance

**Redirect:** Set up a 301 redirect from /get-involved/partner/ to /partner/ to preserve any existing inbound links and SEO value.

### Language Changes Across the Site

Audit all instances of "sponsor" and "sponsorship" and replace with "partner" and "partnership" where appropriate. Some exceptions:

- **"Trail sponsor"** can remain as a tier name — it's well-established and the term "trail partner" doesn't convey the same "this trail was funded by..." meaning
- **"Sponsorship@namba.ngo" email** — Consider migrating to partnership@namba.ngo (set up both, redirect the old one)
- **Pitch deck content** — Update language in the next version of the deck
- **Signage** — Physical trailhead signs say "Trail Sponsor" — these don't need to change (they're already installed and the term is clear in context)

The key places to update: website nav, partner page heading and body copy, homepage CTAs, LinkedIn messaging, email communications, and the downloadable pitch deck.
