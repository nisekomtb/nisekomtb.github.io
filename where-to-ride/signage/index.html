A unified trail signage system

NAMBA designs and installs a unified trail signage system across all Niseko riding areas. Whether you're riding Twin Peaks, Grand Hirafu, or Hanazono, the signs work the same way. This consistent wayfinding means you can move between parks and always know where you are, how difficult the trail is, and where it goes.

Photo: Close-up of a NAMBA trail sign post showing all elements (trail name, difficulty, sponsor, QR code, direction)

Reading a trail sign

  • Trail name: The name of the trail you're on.
  • Trail difficulty: Rated according to IMBA's international trail difficulty system:
    • Green circle (Easy): Suitable for beginners.
    • Blue square (More Difficult): Suitable for intermediate riders.
    • Black diamond (Very Difficult): For experienced riders.
    • Double black diamond (Extremely Difficult): Expert riders only.
  • Trail direction: Indicates whether the trail is one-way, two-way, downhill only, or uphill only. Always follow the indicated direction.
  • Trail users: Shows whether the trail is bikes-only or multi-use (shared with hikers and trail runners). On multi-use trails, give way to walkers and announce your approach.
  • Location code: An alphanumeric code identifying your position on the trail. Useful for meeting friends or communicating your location to emergency services.
  • QR code: Scan for detailed trail information, interactive maps, and live conditions via Trailforks.
  • Sponsor: Acknowledges the partner who funded the building of the trail.
  • NAMBA: Identifies NAMBA as the organisation responsible for managing and maintaining the trail network.

Difficulty ratings explained

  • Green (Easy): Wide trails, gentle gradients, smooth surfaces. Suitable for beginners and families.
  • Blue (More Difficult): Narrower trails, steeper gradients, uneven surfaces. Requires basic mountain biking skills.
  • Black (Very Difficult): Technical terrain, steep gradients, obstacles. Demands strong skills and experience.
  • Double Black (Extremely Difficult): Highly technical, very steep terrain. Large drops, rock features, and exposure. Expert riders only.

Across the Niseko network

The unified signage system is one of the ways NAMBA connects the riding experience across the region. The same design language appears at Twin Peaks, Grand Hirafu, and Hanazono, creating a cohesive destination experience. As the network grows, every new trail and park will use the same system.
