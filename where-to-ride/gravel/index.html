Gravel Riding in Niseko

Niseko isn't just mountain biking. The area has well over 1,200km of gravel roads, from agricultural paths and forest roads to scenic rural routes winding through farmland beneath Mt Yotei. Hokkaido has the highest density of unpaved roads in Japan, and the Niseko area is one of the best places to ride them.

The gravel network

The gravel roads around Niseko fall into two main types. Agricultural roads (nogyo-do) are the flat, well-maintained paths connecting potato and rice farmland. Forest roads (rindo) head into the hills with more climbing and technical surfaces. Combining both creates routes for every type of rider.

Area highlights

  • Rankoshi: Widely considered the gravel capital of the region. The most extensive network of accessible forest roads, including the Mena River loops and a wealth of trails around Mt Konbu.
  • Makkari & Kimobetsu: Fast gravel around the base of Mt Yotei. Flatter, well-maintained agricultural paths ideal for speed and distance.
  • Kutchan: More technical forest climbs heading into the mountains toward the Sea of Japan side. For the more adventurous rider.

The riding

  • Distance: Routes ranging from 20km casual rides to 80km+ full-day adventures.
  • Difficulty: Agricultural roads are mostly flat and beginner-friendly. Forest roads offer more climbing and rougher surfaces for a bigger challenge.
  • Scenery: Hokkaido's volcanic landscape at its finest. Farmland, forests, rivers, and mountain panoramas. Quiet roads with minimal traffic.

Niseko Gravel events

The Niseko Gravel series runs spring and autumn rides through the area's gravel roads, a celebration of Hokkaido's landscape and the gravel riding community. Check our events page for upcoming dates.

Gear & preparation

  • Bike: Gravel bike recommended. A mountain bike or cyclocross bike will work on most routes. Drop bars with wider tyres (35mm+) are ideal.
  • Essentials: Spare tube, multi-tool, pump, water, snacks. Mobile coverage can be patchy on remote routes.
  • Navigation: A GPS device or phone with offline maps is recommended. Routes are not signposted like the MTB trails.
  • Season: Best from May to October. Spring and autumn offer cooler temperatures and stunning colours.
